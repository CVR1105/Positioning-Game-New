1. Objective

Make the most money (points). Your team is the marketing department of a firm, and you are paid to maximise profit. The winner is the team with the highest accumulated profit after the final round (8 rounds by default).

Accumulated profit = 5,000 starting points + Σ (sales − positioning costs − penalties) over all rounds

The game looks simple, but like any strategy game it rewards understanding competitor behaviour and the payback (ROI) on marketing investment.

2. Quick start

Teams (players)

  1. Open the link your facilitator shares, or go to the home page and type the 6-character game code.
  2. Enter a team name and a team PIN. Teammates on other laptops enter the same name and PIN to join the same team.
  3. Wait in the lobby until the facilitator starts round 1.
  4. Each round: plan at least one move on the map, check the cost, and click Submit.
  5. Watch the market simulation, study the results, and get ready for the next round.

Facilitator

  1. Home → Host a game. Choose a PIN, rounds, timers and options.
  2. Share the team link (or code) shown in the console. Open the 📺 Projector view on the classroom screen.
  3. When the teams have joined, click Start round 1.
  4. Rounds process automatically once everyone has submitted, or you can click Process round.
  5. Click Open round N after each results screen. After round 8, click Reveal final leaderboard.

No account or app is needed. The game runs in any modern browser on laptops, tablets and phones.

3. The market: perceptual map & segments

Consumers choose mainly on two attributes: quality (columns 1→10, left to right) and style/look (rows J→A, bottom to top). Each cell holds a fixed amount of market demand (points of sales available per round). Think clothing, shoes, furniture or accessories.

The four key segments (demand peaks)

  • A10: Status Seekers (1,500). Best quality and best style, and happy to pay for it. The largest segment, with attractive neighbouring cells (A9 1,000, B10 1,000). Also the most expensive to reach and potentially the most crowded: it can become a profit trap.
  • D3: Fashion Conscious (800). Want good-looking, socially acceptable products and will give up some quality to pay less.
  • H3: Budget Conscious (1,000). Accept lower quality and basic style for an affordable product. Close to START, so cheap to reach.
  • I8: Quality Focused (800). Want high quality and care less about style or fashion.

J1 is START: the free, basic generic product with no demand. Every product is developed from J1, and products in J1 sell nothing.

Tip: avoid choosing cells on gut feel. Compare profit opportunity, cost to reach, payback time, and what competitors are likely to do.

4. Your decisions each round

Every round, your team can take any combination of these actions:

ActionWhat it doesCost
🚀 Launch a new productDevelops a new product from START (J1) and positions it in any cell except J1.200 × cells from J1
↔️ Reposition an existing productMoves one of your products to another cell. You can move several cells in one round. Its loyalty bonus resets to 0.200 × cells moved
🛡️ Hold a productLeave it where it is. It builds loyalty (+10% compounding). Holding by itself does not count as a move.Free
✕ Withdraw a productPermanently removes it from the market (see section 8).Double penalty

Rules

  • HOUSE RULE At least one move is compulsory every round (launch, reposition or withdraw). You cannot submit "no move". The only exception: if your budget is below 200, no move is affordable, so you may submit without one.
  • HOUSE RULE A launched product cannot be removed or deleted. It stays in your portfolio for the rest of the game. The only way out is a formal withdrawal with the double penalty. Before you submit, you can still cancel a planned launch freely, because it has not been launched yet.
  • Maximum 20 products per team over the whole game. Withdrawn products still use up a slot.
  • Total cost this round ≤ your budget, where your budget is your accumulated profit. In round 1 that is 5,000.
  • Moves are horizontal or vertical only, never diagonal. Cost is the number of cells moved, even when you reduce quality or style, because you must redesign the product and re-educate the market.
  • You can change your plan, and even unlock a submitted decision with Edit decision, until the round is processed.
  • Product numbers (#1, #2…) are fixed automatically. Unlike the paper decision sheet, you can't be charged by mistake for listing products in a different order.

Using the decision screen

  • Click 🚀 Launch new product, then click a cell on the map. You can also click any empty cell directly.
  • Click one of your product chips on the map, or Move in the list, then click the target cell. Click its current cell to cancel.
  • Hover over any cell to see its demand, how many products were there last round, the cost to reach it, and an estimate of your sales if you enter.
  • The panel on the right shows your budget, the cost of this round and what remains. Submit stays disabled until the decision is valid.
  • Your plan autosaves, so teammates on other laptops see the same plan.

5. Positioning costs (200 points per cell)

Launch cost = 200 × ( |row − J| + |column − 1| ) Reposition cost = 200 × ( |row change| + |column change| )

Cost to launch a brand-new product in each cell (distance from J1 × 200):

Example: launch in H3 (Budget Conscious). Up 2 rows (J→H) and across 2 columns (1→3) = 4 cells × 200 = 800. The cell has 1,000 demand, so if you are alone there it pays back in round 1.
Example: reposition D7 → A10. Up 3 rows and across 3 columns = 6 cells × 200 = 1,200.
Example: launch in A10 (Status Seekers). 9 rows + 9 columns = 18 cells × 200 = 3,600. Alone, it earns 1,500 in its first round, 1,650 in the next (with loyalty), and so on. If 3 teams pile in, each earns only 500, and payback takes 7 or more rounds.

6. How sales are calculated

The demand of a cell is shared equally between all products positioned there (from all teams, including your own), then the product's loyalty bonus is added:

Sales of a product = ( Cell demand ÷ number of products in that cell ) × ( 1 + loyalty bonus )
  • Alone in a 1,000-point cell: 1,000.
  • Two products in a 1,000-point cell: 500 each. Five products: 200 each.
  • Two of your own products in the same 1,000-point cell with no competitors: 500 each, 1,000 in total. The second product earns nothing extra, but it makes the cell much less attractive to competitors, who would only get a third. This is a blocking strategy.

Sales are rounded to whole points. Team sales are the sum of all your products' sales.

7. The loyalty & price-premium bonus (10% compounding)

Products that stay in the same cell build customer loyalty, purchase frequency and price premium. For every consecutive round a product is held (not launched, not moved), its bonus compounds at 10%:

Loyalty bonus = 1.10 ^ (rounds held in a row) − 1
  • A product's first round (new launch) and any round in which it is repositioned has a 0% bonus. The count restarts from zero.
  • The bonus applies to the product's equal share of the cell. When a competitor enters your cell, your share halves but you keep your bonus multiplier, so established products still out-sell newcomers.
Example. Your product is alone in a 500-point cell.
Round 1 (new): 500 × 1.00 = 500
Round 2 (held 1): 500 × 1.10 = 550
Round 3 (held 2), and a competitor enters: (500 ÷ 2) × 1.21 = 303 for you, while the newcomer earns 250 × 1.00 = 250.

This is the 2020 version of the rule (10% compounding, reaching about 95% by round 8), which replaced the older 20% bonus because that rewarded inactive teams too much.

8. Withdrawing a product: the double penalty HOUSE RULE

Launched products cannot be deleted. If you formally withdraw one, you pay a double penalty:

Lifetime P/L of product = lifetime sales − lifetime positioning costs (launch + every move) If the product is loss-making (lifetime P/L < 0): Penalty = 2 × lifetime loss ← the loss is deducted twice Otherwise: Penalty = 2 × (200 × cells back to J1) ← double the original sheet's withdrawal cost
Loss-making example. Product #2 was launched in A10 for 3,600. It earned 750 and then 825 (shared with a rival), so lifetime sales are 1,575 and the lifetime loss is 2,025. Withdrawing it costs 2 × 2,025 = 4,050.
Profitable example. Product #1 is in H3. It cost 800 and has earned 3,000. It is not loss-making, so the penalty is 2 × (4 cells × 200) = 1,600.
  • The penalty counts toward your round's total cost and must fit within your budget.
  • A withdrawal counts as your compulsory move for the round.
  • The slot stays used (it still counts toward the 20-product limit), and the product earns nothing from the round it is withdrawn.
  • The decision screen shows the exact penalty and asks you to confirm before it is added to your plan.
  • Repositioning a weak product is almost always cheaper than withdrawing it.

9. Profit, budget & winning

Round costs = positioning costs + withdrawal penalties Round net profit = round sales − round costs Accumulated profit = previous accumulated profit + round net profit (starts at 5,000) Next round budget = accumulated profit
  • Spending is limited to your accumulated profit, so your accumulated profit can never fall below zero.
  • Investment needs payback. Near the end of the game there are fewer rounds left to recover a launch or a move, so a 3,600 launch in round 7 rarely pays off.
  • Teams are ranked each round by net profit and by accumulated profit. Equal values share a rank.
  • The team with the highest accumulated profit after the final round wins.

10. How a round works

  1. Lobby. Teams join with the link or code. The facilitator starts round 1.
  2. Decision phase. Every team plans and submits privately. You see which teams have submitted (✓) but never what they chose. If the facilitator set a timer, a countdown appears; when it hits zero the round is processed.
  3. Processing. The server applies every team's moves at the same time, counts the products in each cell, calculates sales with loyalty bonuses, deducts costs and penalties, and ranks the teams.
  4. Results. Every laptop plays the market simulation and then shows the round results, leaderboard, charts, market map and news ticker.
  5. The facilitator (or the results timer) opens the next round. This repeats until round 8.
  6. Final leaderboard. After round 8 the facilitator reveals the podium and full ranking. Every round's results stay available.

Missed submission: if a team has not submitted when the round is processed (timer or facilitator), its products stay where they are with no move and no cost. Its held products still earn and build loyalty. The results mark that team as "no submission".

Reliability: the server stores every round, so a refresh, a closed lid or a dropped Wi-Fi connection never loses progress. Open the link again and you are back where you were.

11. Reading the results screen

  • Your KPIs: sales, market share, costs, net profit, accumulated profit and overall rank, with the change since last round.
  • Market news ticker: leader changes, big climbers, crowded or empty segments, and the best-selling product.
  • Leaderboard: every team's sales, share, best product, costs, net profit, accumulated profit and number of products, plus the total market and the average team.
  • Charts (each team in its own colour): accumulated profit (the key metric), sales per round, profit per round and market share. Hover over a chart to see exact values.
  • Competitive map: where every product sits, shown as team-coloured dots, plus the most contested cells. On the next decision screen, the ×n badges show how many products each cell held last round.
  • Your products: for each product, its status (new, repositioned or held), the number of products in its cell, its equal share, its loyalty bonus, its sales and its cost this round.
  • Top 10 products and product sales by team: spot the winners to attack, and the products of yours to protect.
  • My portfolio tab: each product's full path, lifetime sales, cost and P/L, and your private decision history.

12. What other teams can and cannot see

Private to your team

  • Your plan and decision while the round is open
  • Your list of actions and their individual costs
  • Your decision history
  • Your team PIN

Public after each round

  • Whether a team has submitted (not what they chose)
  • Each team's sales, costs, profit, accumulated profit and rank
  • Market results by cell: product counts and, if the facilitator enabled it (the default, as in the original game), each product's cell and sales

The server enforces this. Another team's browser never receives your decisions, so they can't be found even with developer tools. Only the facilitator can see decisions during a round.

13. Strategy tips

  • Think in ROI and payback. Payback in rounds ≈ cost ÷ expected sales per round. Early rounds give the most time to recover big investments.
  • Cheap segments near START (H3, G3, I8) can return their cost in a single round when they are uncontested.
  • Beware the profit trap. A10 is huge, but if four teams go there each gets 375 a round for a 3,600 investment.
  • Predict competitors. Use the ×n badges, the leaderboard and the top 10 list to work out where rivals will move next.
  • Protect your stars. Held products compound loyalty, and a second product in the same cell can deter entrants.
  • Reposition, don't withdraw. Moving a weak product one or two cells to an empty neighbour usually beats paying the double penalty.
  • The compulsory move can be cheap: a single 200-point move or a cheap launch into an empty low-cost cell still counts.
  • Late game: in rounds 7 and 8, favour moves that pay back within the remaining rounds.

14. Facilitator guide

Creating a game

  • Facilitator PIN: needed to reopen the console from another device. Keep it secret.
  • Rounds (1–8) and maximum teams (1–10).
  • Decision timer: optional countdown per round. When it ends, the round is processed automatically.
  • Results timer: optional. Opens the next round automatically after N minutes of results. Set it to 0 to control the pace yourself.
  • Auto-process: calculates the round as soon as every team has submitted.
  • Show competitors' positions: on by default, as in the original game. Turn it off for a harder, lower-information game; teams then see only totals and cell counts.

During the game

  • The console shows who is online, who has submitted, and every team's decision (visible only to you).
  • +1 min, 5:00 and Stop timer control the countdown for the current phase.
  • Process round calculates the round now. Teams that have not submitted hold their products.
  • Open round N starts the next decision phase. After the last round, Reveal final leaderboard ends the game.
  • Reset PIN (in the lobby list) helps a team that forgot its PIN.
  • ⚙️ Settings lets you change timers and options mid-game.
  • ⬇ CSV / JSON exports every round's results and decisions for grading.
  • 📺 Projector view is a read-only big-screen view with the live submission status, leaderboard and podium.

Buttons are protected against double clicks and stale tabs, so a round can never be skipped or processed twice.

15. Complete game logic

This is the exact algorithm the server runs, from engine.js, the same file that powers the cost previews in your browser.

Constants

Grid rows A..J (A = best style), columns 1..10 (10 = best quality); START = J1 (demand 0) Starting accumulated profit = 5,000 Cost per cell moved = 200 Max products per team = 20 Loyalty rate = 10% compounding Rounds = 8 (configurable 1-8) Teams = up to 10

Validation when a team submits

for each action: launch(cell): cell valid and ≠ J1; cost = 200 × dist(J1, cell) move(#p, cell): #p active, cell ≠ current cell, cell ≠ J1; cost = 200 × dist(current, cell) withdraw(#p): #p active; cost = penalty(#p) (section 8) each product can appear in at most one action reject if products launched so far + new launches > 20 reject if total cost > accumulated profit reject if no actions AND accumulated profit ≥ 200 ← compulsory move dist(a, b) = |row_a − row_b| + |col_a − col_b| (no diagonals)

Processing a round (all teams at the same time)

1. For every team apply its decision (or nothing if it did not submit): launch → new product #(n+1) in cell, lifetime cost = launch cost, held = 0 move → product changes cell, lifetime cost += move cost, held = 0 withdraw → product status = withdrawn (permanently), penalty charged 2. count[cell] = number of active products in each cell (all teams) 3. For every active product: held = 0 if launched or moved this round, else held + 1 bonus = 1.10^held − 1 sales = round( demand[cell] / count[cell] × (1 + bonus) ) lifetime sales += sales 4. For every team: sales = Σ product sales costs = Σ positioning costs + Σ penalties profit = sales − costs accumulated profit += profit market share = team sales / total market sales 5. Rank teams by profit and by accumulated profit (ties share a rank), compute the market totals and averages, the top 10 products and the news headlines. 6. Save everything; every connected screen updates instantly.

16. FAQ & troubleshooting

Do players need an account or Claude subscription? No. Anyone with the link can play in a normal browser.

Our laptop refreshed or crashed. Did we lose everything? No. Reopen the link. Your team stays logged in on that browser, and on a new laptop you just enter your team name and PIN.

Can two laptops play as the same team? Yes. Use the same team name and PIN. Both see the same products, plan and submission.

Why is the Submit button disabled? You need at least one move, and the total cost must fit your budget. Any errors are listed above the button.

Can we change our mind after submitting? Yes. Click Edit decision until the round is processed. If auto-process is on and you are the last team to submit, the round processes immediately.

The status shows "Reconnecting". Your connection dropped. The page retries automatically every few seconds, and nothing is lost.

Can new teams join mid-game? No. Joining is only possible in the lobby, so the game stays fair. Existing teams can always rejoin.
